The problem

The bank needed digital channels that could keep up with national payment schemes, digital wallets and a growing list of institutional clients, without risking the trust of hundreds of thousands of retail customers. Every new capability had to meet central bank ICT guidelines and fit around the bank’s core banking system.

What we built

One platform with four channels on top. Around 35 backend services and 10 portals sit behind it, connected to the core banking system and the country’s payment, wallet, telecom and identity infrastructure.

What customers can do

  • Move money: own-account and in-bank transfers, other banks over BEFTN and NPSB, bKash and Nagad wallets, and Bangla QR, including QR-to-QR transfer and QR cash withdrawal.
  • Pay: mobile recharge on all five operators, credit card bills, tuition and school fees, loan repayments, and online purchases with external merchants through the bank’s payment gateway.
  • Recurring: Standing Instructions for scheme instalments and fixed-amount transfers, for all of the bank’s customers.
  • Corporate: 500+ institutional customers on corporate banking, including bulk disbursement of payments from one instruction.
  • Manage: statements and downloads, beneficiaries, device changes, disputes, and a chatbot. Accounts open through self-service eKYC or with help from a branch.
  • EduPay: students register with their university, pay fees through ShurjoPay or the bank’s own gateway, and download receipts.

One process, end to end

An interbank transfer looks simple to the customer. Behind it are the core banking system, a national payment network and a back-office team that makes sure every transfer settles exactly once.

How we delivered

We run Scrum and plan sprints at an 80% velocity factor against roughly 85% realised capacity, leaving room for production support and regulatory change. The backlog uses feature IDs, a definition of ready, T-shirt sizing and a flag for whether test cases are written. Every feature moves through the same stages: sprint, QA, UAT, production. Compliance requirements, from Bangladesh Bank’s ICT security guidelines to the applicable PCI-DSS controls and PII protection, are written into each PRD’s definition of done and checked before release. Every feature shipped so far went live on time, within scope and within budget.

Each service ships through a GitLab pipeline that builds, deploys, health-checks and rolls back automatically if the check fails, with SonarQube on the way. Web journeys are tested with Cypress and the mobile app with Appium and WebdriverIO; the app alone has hundreds of automated tests. Standard releases take fifteen minutes, down from an hour. Releases that need downtime are agreed with the bank in advance and announced to customers.
