I run engineering and product for platforms where unannounced downtime isn’t a technical event. It’s a trust event.

Right now that means a state-owned bank’s digital banking platform in Bangladesh: retail internet banking for 640,000+ customers, corporate banking for 500+ institutional customers, a branch and back-office portal for 1,500+ staff, and education fee collection for universities. Sixteen engineers report to me across backend, web, mobile, QA, production support and data. I own hiring, performance and budget, as well as the roadmap and the client relationship.

Before that I spent about seven years building systems for five regulators: the central bank, tax, insurance, telecom and microcredit. I started as an engineer and moved into management because the constraint on shipping was never the code. I still stay close to the work through requirements, PRDs, production incidents and the project’s cloud and on-premises infrastructure. I also introduced an agentic SDLC for my team and my own PM work, and I led the platform’s UK expansion project with UK stakeholders.

How I deliver

  1. Discover

    I sit with the regulator or the bank team, read the circular, and turn it into PRDs and acceptance criteria that an engineer and an auditor can both check.

  2. Plan

    We run Scrum and plan at an 80% velocity factor against roughly 85% realised capacity. That headroom is for production support and regulatory change, which always arrive mid-sprint. Every two sprints we hold a backlog refinement session so everyone stays aligned on what comes next.

  3. Build

    Test-driven where it pays, with 90% automated coverage across backend, web and mobile. That coverage saves about 25 hours of manual QA a week. An agentic SDLC: AI coding agents help with codebase analysis, code review, Jira backlog work and delivery documents, and a person reviews everything before it is used or shipped.

  4. Ship

    Standard releases take fifteen minutes, down from an hour. Releases that need downtime are agreed with the client in advance and announced to customers.

  5. Run

    Observability on Prometheus, Grafana, Graylog and Zipkin, so we find incidents before customers do. Stakeholders and the Managing Director get a daily update: what happened, the impact, what is being done, who owns the next action and when it will be resolved. When a project finishes its build, I set it up to keep running under a sustain contract, with a team that owns it.
